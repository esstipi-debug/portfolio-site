Case Study — Safety Stock & EOQ

How do you calculate safety stock, reorder point and EOQ?

Safety stock is z times the standard deviation of demand over the lead time, where z comes from the service level you choose: 1.65 for 95%. The reorder point is expected demand over the lead time plus that buffer. EOQ is the square root of 2DK/h — the order size where ordering cost and holding cost balance. The mistake to avoid is sizing safety stock as a flat percentage of demand.

Worked example

Demand over the lead time averages 100 units, with a standard deviation of 25. The target is a 95% cycle service level, so z = 1.65. Annual demand is 1,000 units, each order costs €50 to place, and holding one unit for a year costs €1.75.

41Safety stock (units): demand over the lead time μ=100, σ=25, 95% service level
141Reorder point (units): 100 + 41
239EOQ (units): D=1,000/yr, K=€50/order, h=€1.75/unit/yr
€418Ordering plus holding cost per year at that quantity

Safety stock = 1.65 × 25 = 41. Reorder point = 100 + 41 = 141. EOQ = √(2 × 1,000 × 50 / 1.75) = 239. Every number on this page can be checked with a calculator.

What the shortcuts cost

Same scenario, two shortcuts people actually use.

79%Service level actually delivered by the “20% of demand” rule, against a 95% target
29%More per year from ordering a round lot of 500 instead of 239

“Safety stock = 20% of demand” gives 20 units. That is a z of 0.8, which is a 79% service level — you think you are covered in 19 cycles out of 20, and you are covered in about 16. A lot of 500 costs €538 a year instead of €418, every year, for a number chosen because it was round.

Where this doesn’t work

When the lead time varies. The formula above assumes the supplier is punctual. When the lead time swings, the buffer needs a second term — average demand squared times the lead time variance — and on most SKUs that term is larger than the demand term. On intermittent demand. The standard deviation of a series that is mostly zeros misrepresents the risk. Use a policy built for it. When there is an MOQ or a quantity discount. EOQ assumes neither. With a minimum above the EOQ, compare total cost at both quantities and take that number to the supplier.

And the service level here is cycle service level — the chance of not running out in a cycle. It is not fill rate, which is usually the number people mean.
