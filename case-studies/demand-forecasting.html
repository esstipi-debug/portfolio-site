Case Study — Forecasting

How do you forecast SKUs with intermittent demand?

Classify before you forecast. Segment the catalogue by value (ABC) and by demand variability (XYZ), send intermittent SKUs — long runs of zero with the occasional order — to Croston’s method instead of a moving average, and measure the result against naive persistence: last period’s value carried forward. If a forecast can’t beat that, it isn’t one.

Why it matters

A moving average on an intermittent SKU spreads the zeros into a small positive number every period. It under-forecasts the order when it arrives and holds stock in between. Every number downstream — safety stock, reorder point, the purchase order — inherits that error.

The evidence

~19%Lower forecast error than naive persistence (MAE and demand-weighted WAPE), 100 M5 SKUs, held-out actuals
99/100Sampled SKUs classified as intermittent and routed to Croston’s method

On one metric naive wins: unweighted per-SKU average WAPE, by 3.2 points. That is a known artifact of the metric on intermittent demand — an SKU that sells 2 units weighs the same as one that sells 2,000, and percentage errors blow up near zero. It is why the M5 competition itself does not score on plain per-series WAPE. Report both, and say which one you are optimising.

At scale

The same classify → forecast → inventory-policy pipeline ran end to end across 75 public retail datasets — M5 by store and department, UCI Online Retail and Kaggle Superstore. Public data, not client data.

Where this doesn’t work

New SKUs with no history. There is nothing to classify. Forecast from a comparable SKU or from the launch plan, and switch once a few months of sales exist. Demand driven by a handful of large customers. Croston treats order size as noise. If three accounts drive the volume, their order calendar beats any model. Histories with stockouts and promotions left in. A stockout period recorded as zero sales teaches the model that demand fell, and it will underbuy the exact SKUs that keep running out. Clean the history before you trust any method on it.

How it’s built

Kern’s forecasting engine, in Python and pandas: ABC-XYZ segmentation, Croston for intermittent series, following Vandeput (2020), chapter 9. Routing between methods is deterministic.
